# Growth & Retention Strategy

## Use when

- Pricing or packaging is being decided.
- A launch plan, beta plan, or go-to-market is being drafted.
- A retention or monetization loop is being designed.
- Investor / partner / press framing is being prepared.
- A competitor announcement requires a response.

## Required framing

1. **Who** — segment, named in their own words.
2. **Wedge** — single problem solved better than any alternative for that
   segment.
3. **Honest alternative they use today** — "spreadsheet", "nothing",
   "competitor X". Not the flattering version.
4. **Price hypothesis** — anchored to what the segment already pays for
   the alternative.
5. **Channel** — where the segment is reachable, rough CAC.
6. **Retention loop** — what brings them back in week two and week six.
7. **Anti-scope** — explicit list of what we will NOT do.

## Pricing discipline

- Anchor against the alternative, not arbitrary tiers.
- Free tier only if it produces network effects, qualified leads, or
  genuine word-of-mouth.
- Annual discount only if it reduces real churn cost.

## Beta / launch discipline

- Closed beta has a stated learning goal, not "feedback".
- Public launch waits until beta cohort hits acceptable activation and
  week-2 retention.
- "Coming soon" pages collect intent, not signatures.

## Output

```
## Decision under review
## Recommendation
## Who (segment, in their words)
## Wedge
## Honest alternative
## Price hypothesis
## Channel (with rough CAC)
## Retention loop
## Anti-scope
## Investor / partner one-liner
## Risks and what would change this recommendation
```

## Hard rules

- No TAM/SAM/SOM theater without bottom-up calculation.
- No "viral" without a specific mechanism.
- No "10x better" without naming the 1x baseline.
- If you cannot name the segment in their own words, the strategy is
  premature — say so.
